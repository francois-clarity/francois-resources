The Procrastination Pattern Breaker

One task, taken apart, right here on this page. You are not managing time badly. There is an ache inside the task, and every delay is medicating it. This finds the ache, then breaks the pattern.

The tool below comes from this video. Watch it first if you can: the questions will land differently once you know what they are pointing at.

How this works. Six short steps, one task. Type your answers right here, on your phone or laptop; the page keeps them as you go. Do not do ten tasks. One. At the end you get everything you wrote on one page, and you can download it as a PDF to keep.

Step 1 of 6

Name the task plainly

Not "sort my life out". That is a fog machine. Write it the way you would say it to a friend: "send the proposal", "twenty minutes on the budget", "ask my partner for a conversation".

Step 2 of 6

Name the feeling

Ask: "What shows up when I imagine doing this?" Do not analyse it. Just name it. Tap what answers, or write your own.

Step 3 of 6

Find the pattern

Complete these honestly. The task has not changed. Your relationship with the task has changed. That is what we are repairing.

Step 4 of 6

Choose a contact action of less than ten minutes

A system that has avoided a task for six months does not trust a five-hour plan. So we go small. The question is not "can I finish this now?" The question is "can I make honest contact now?" Open the document. Write one bad paragraph. Make the first call. Look at the bank account.

Step 5 of 6

Decide the next action before you stop

The step everyone skips. Do not end your contact with vague hope. End it with a visible next step, written down. Say when, where and how, and a vague desire becomes an instruction.

Step 6 of 6

Afterwards, ask the right question

Not "did this change my life?" Wrong question. Ask: "Did I interrupt the loop?" That is the first win. You repair self-trust the way you repair trust with anyone: small promises, kept consistently.
