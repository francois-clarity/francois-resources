You already know what to do.

You've built something that works. Somewhere along the way, it started depending on you for almost everything.

You know you should hand more over, stop putting out every fire yourself, get what's in your head written down, and finish work before your family has gone to bed. You've probably known for a while.

And yet here you are, a year later, still the person everything waits on. If you can't step away from your business for two weeks, you are the bottleneck. And it isn't only costing you revenue and freedom. It's a tax on your health and the relationships that matter to you.

Nothing's wrong with you. The habits that built your business are the ones keeping you stuck in it now. Your belief system has been practising to keep you safe for years. It's sneaky and clever, and it's good at its job.

Reading about it only takes you so far, because the belief lives in your everyday choices: the email you answer yourself, the task you take back, the evening you stay late. That's where you have to catch it. That's what WOEMA is for: three hours of finding it in your own business, with me there to point out what you'd otherwise explain away.

Three steps, in order.

  1. 1

    Understand your own potential.

    We start with what your business and your life could look like if you weren't holding everything up. That picture comes first, because it's what we measure everything else against.

  2. 2

    Find what's blocking it.

    Then we look at the gap between what you know you should do as a leader and what you actually do on a Tuesday. There's usually a belief sitting in that gap, often an old one that made perfect sense when you learned it. "Nobody does it as well as I do." "I have to be available for everything." "If I slow down, I'll fall behind." We find yours and put it into plain words.

  3. 3

    Reshape your paradigm.

    By that I mean the set of beliefs you look at your business and your life through. Once a belief is written down, you can question it and see what it has been costing you. Before we finish, you choose the first small action that starts building evidence for something different.

So you can get out of your own way.

What you walk away with

Your bottleneck

The pattern, named.

Whether you hold on to the work, fight every fire, carry all the knowledge or run on effort alone, you'll know which belief keeps that pattern running, and where you learned it.

Delegation

One thing off your plate.

You leave having chosen the task, decision or piece of knowledge you're handing over first, who it goes to, and what "done" looks like.

Your week

A finish line.

You leave with the hours you're protecting this week: for the work only you can do, and for the people at home who've been getting what's left of you.

All three on one page, in your own handwriting, before we finish.
