Affairs are not built out of attraction. They are built out of secrecy.

Almost nobody decides to have an affair. What happens instead is architectural, and it happens slowly. A wall goes up between two people who used to tell each other things, and somewhere else a window opens.

Shirley Glass spent two decades studying how that happens, and her finding was that the slide runs in an order: a friendship, then a closer friendship, then something emotional, and only then anything physical. People who never intended any of it are already deep in before they notice a line was crossed.

So this asks nothing about who you find attractive. Being attracted to people is not a character flaw and it predicts nothing. It asks where the walls and windows in your relationship currently sit.

The thing that makes this worth doing twice

Give this to your partner and let them do it on their own phone, without discussing it first. Then compare the two houses. The gap between how each of you drew it is the actual conversation, and it is a far better one than "is everything alright between us".

Nothing above is a verdict on your marriage. A wall in the wrong place is not a betrayal, it is a thing that drifted, and drifted things can be moved back on purpose. That is the whole reason to look at it while it is still boring.

What this is built on Shirley Glass's work on emotional affairs, which describes the intimacy between a couple as a window and the protection around them as a wall, and found that in an affair the two swap places.
