The Unstuck Loop Map

Stuckness is rarely one big problem. It is usually a loop. Map yours right here on this page, then find the point where it can be interrupted.

The map below comes from this video. Watch it first if you can: the loop will be much easier to see in your own life once you have seen how it works.

Discomfort asks for movement. Avoidance buys relief. Relief teaches your nervous system to reach for avoidance again. And the cost keeps growing. Five sentences on this page map your loop; one small action interrupts it.

Step 1 of 7 · Write five sentences

What do I want?

Be concrete. Not "better". Name it.

Step 2 of 7

What discomfort shows up when I move towards it?

Write the feeling, not the essay.

Step 3 of 7

What do I do to avoid that discomfort?

Tap what fits, or write your own. Avoidance rarely looks like lying on the couch. It usually looks busy.

Step 4 of 7

What relief do I get from avoiding?

Be honest about the payoff. Avoidance survives because it gives you something.

Step 5 of 7

What does that relief cost me?

Self-trust first. Then time, intimacy, health, opportunity.

Step 6 of 7

What fuels your loop?

Tick the one or two that fuel the loop you just mapped.

Pick one or two. More than two usually means one of them is the real driver hiding behind the others.

Step 7 of 7 · Your five-minute interruption

When I feel discomfort, I make contact for five minutes.

The purpose of this action is not transformation. The purpose is evidence.

Remember: a system that has been stuck for months does not trust a five-hour plan. Five minutes of honest contact interrupts the loop. That is the whole job today.

Watch for three false exits
  1. More insight without action. Understanding names the pattern; action interrupts it.
  2. Intensity. A plan that cannot survive an ordinary Tuesday is not a plan yet.
  3. Waiting for confidence. Confidence is the result of movement, not the entrance fee.
